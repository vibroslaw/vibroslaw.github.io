WHAT YOU ARE ABOUT TO WATCH

A guide to music, image and historical memory.

RAP-ORT: Truth of Conscience is an audiovisual work shaped by music, image, testimony and historical memory. It uses artistic form to help the viewer approach a difficult record without pretending to replace historical study.

The guide exists after the screening because emotion alone is not enough. It offers orientation for English-speaking viewers, including the Polish diaspora in Sydney, so the story can be understood beyond Poland as a question about conscience, responsibility and public memory.

01

Memory

The work returns to a record made under extreme pressure and asks how memory is carried across generations, languages and countries.

02

Testimony

In this project, report means testimony, responsibility and moral record. It is not only a document, but a demand placed on the receiver.

03

Reflection

The guide separates historical context from artistic interpretation so the viewer can leave with a clearer question, not a simplified answer.

BEFORE YOU WATCH

Seven orientation cards for non-specialists.

These notes are intentionally brief. They prepare the viewer without turning the guide into an encyclopedia.

IPersonWho was Witold Pilecki?Witold Pilecki was a Polish cavalry officer and resistance member. During the German occupation of Poland, he voluntarily entered KL Auschwitz under an assumed identity to organize resistance and transmit reports that became part of the historical record.
IIPlaceWhy Auschwitz matters here?The work turns around KL Auschwitz, the German Nazi concentration and extermination camp Auschwitz in occupied Poland. It is the place where testimony, survival, resistance and mass murder meet in the historical record.
IIIOccupationWhat was occupied Poland?In 1939 Poland was invaded by Germany and the Soviet Union. Civilian life, state institutions and public memory were placed under violent systems of control.
IVResistanceWhat was the Polish Underground State?The Polish Underground State was a clandestine wartime structure of civilian and military resistance. It tried to preserve continuity, information and responsibility under occupation.
VReportsWhy were Pilecki's reports important?The reports connected observation, warning and moral responsibility. They helped carry knowledge from inside the camp into external channels, even when the response remained inadequate.
VIAfter 1945Why was his story suppressed after the war?After the war, Poland came under a Soviet-dominated communist system. Pilecki's memory and the memory of many anti-communist figures were suppressed or distorted in public life.
VIIMeaningWhat does "report" mean in this project?Report means more than an archival document. It means testimony received, responsibility accepted and a moral record that continues in the viewer.

WHY PILECKI MATTERS

A life at the crossing of courage, testimony and memory.

This section gives only the frame needed for the audiovisual work. Fuller study belongs with the institutions listed below.

I

Courage under occupation

Pilecki's wartime story belongs to a wider history of occupied Poland, clandestine resistance and the attempt to record crimes while they were still unfolding.

II

Testimony under pressure

The reports matter because they show testimony as action. The work asks what it means to speak, write and carry evidence when doing so may cost a life.

III

Memory after repression

After the war, his memory was suppressed under the communist system in Poland. After 1989, public memory could return more openly through research, education and commemoration.

TIMELINE: 1901-1948-TODAY

A concise orientation line.

The timeline is not a full biography. It gives the viewer the essential historical path behind the work.

  1. Witold Pilecki is born in Olonets, then within the Russian Empire.

  2. Germany and the Soviet Union invade Poland, beginning occupation and wartime resistance.

  3. Pilecki enters KL Auschwitz as part of his voluntary mission to organize resistance, observe the camp and transmit reports.

  4. An underground network forms in the camp, and reports carry information beyond the wire.

  5. Pilecki escapes and continues the effort to make testimony known.

  6. He takes part in the Warsaw Uprising, another chapter of wartime resistance and loss.

  7. The war ends, but Poland enters a new political order shaped by Soviet domination.

  8. Pilecki is tried and executed by the communist authorities in Poland.

  9. With the fall of communism, his story can return more openly to public memory.

  10. The report continues through memory, education and conscience.

PEOPLE BEHIND THE STORY

Persons and collective memory.

The guide uses restrained language and avoids reducing people to slogans.

WP

Witold Pilecki

Polish officer, resistance member and author of reports connected with KL Auschwitz. In the work, he stands at the centre of testimony, risk and conscience.

JC

Józef Cyrankiewicz

A prisoner of Auschwitz who later became a major political figure in communist Poland. In the work, he appears as a historically sensitive point of contrast around survival, public power and memory.

ZOW

Prisoners, witnesses and the underground

The story is also carried by collective memory: prisoners, witnesses, families, couriers, researchers and the wartime underground structures that tried to preserve truth.

GLOSSARY

Terms for international viewers.

Simple definitions for words and abbreviations used around the history.

AK / Home Army

The main armed resistance force of the Polish Underground State during the Second World War.

ZOW / Union of Military Organization

A clandestine resistance network organized inside Auschwitz by Witold Pilecki and fellow prisoners.

KL / Konzentrationslager

German for concentration camp. Use it carefully as part of the German Nazi camp system terminology.

Auschwitz

The German Nazi concentration and extermination camp Auschwitz in occupied Poland.

Łapanka

A street roundup carried out by German occupation forces, often leading to imprisonment, forced labor or deportation.

PPS

Polish Socialist Party; a political tradition present in modern Polish history and wartime biographies.

PRL

The Polish People's Republic, the communist state system in Poland after the Second World War until 1989.

UB / MBP

Communist security institutions in postwar Poland, connected with political repression.

NKWD / NKVD

The Soviet security police, active in repression across territories controlled by the Soviet Union.

SS

A Nazi organization central to terror, camp administration and mass murder under the German Nazi system.

Polish Underground State

A clandestine wartime structure preserving civil, military and political continuity under occupation.

Rotmistrz

A Polish cavalry rank often translated as cavalry captain.

ZWZ

Union of Armed Struggle, an earlier Polish underground military organization that preceded the Home Army.
