WHAT YOU HAVE ENTERED

A work about testimony under pressure.

Rap-Ort: Prawda Sumienia is an authorial audiovisual work using music, image, archival atmosphere and narrative chapters to approach the moral world around Witold Pilecki's testimony from Auschwitz.

It is not a documentary transcript, not a complete historical reconstruction and not a museum catalogue. It is a cinematic act of memory: a guided encounter with the question of what a human being does when truth becomes dangerous.

01

Historical anchor

The work is anchored in Pilecki's Auschwitz mission, reports, resistance network and later Communist show-trial repression.

02

Artistic language

The score, pacing, visual metaphors, track titles and viewer ritual are interpretive choices, not archival claims.

03

Viewer responsibility

The final station is not applause. It is the private question of what you are willing to carry after testimony reaches you.

FACTS AND INTERPRETATION

A clear line protects both history and art.

Based on historical record

What the work treats as fact

  • Witold Pilecki was a Polish officer and resistance member connected with the Polish Underground State.
  • In 1940 he entered Auschwitz under the false identity of Tomasz Serafinski.
  • Inside the camp he helped organize underground resistance and intelligence gathering connected with ZOW.
  • Reports about Auschwitz moved outward through underground channels before and after his 1943 escape.
  • After the war he was arrested by Communist authorities, sentenced in a show trial and executed on 25 May 1948.
Authorial interpretation

What the work shapes artistically

  • The chapter order is a moral and musical architecture, not a scholarly chronology.
  • The track titles, contrasts, repetitions and visual atmosphere are interpretive devices.
  • The anonymous archive and Witness Report are contemporary post-screening rituals, not historical documents.
  • The work asks ethical questions without claiming to exhaust the history of Auschwitz, occupation or post-war repression.

TOTALITARIAN PRESSURE

Systems try to make conscience smaller.

Totalitarian systems do not only use violence. They isolate people, rename them, number them, reward obedience, punish speech and teach bystanders to survive by looking away.

The work keeps returning to one moral tension: when the system demands silence, who still chooses to see, remember and transmit the truth?

Mechanism Isolation

Separate the person from community, language and witness.

Mechanism Dehumanization

Reduce identity to number, function, fear or accusation.

Moral response Testimony

Notice accurately, remember carefully, speak when speaking costs.

Moral response Solidarity

Keep another human being visible when the system wants them erased.

THE REPORT'S HISTORICAL GRAVITY

A restrained timeline for the viewer.

This is orientation, not a full biography. It marks the historical pressure points needed to understand why the report matters.

1901

Birth of Witold Pilecki

A life formed before the catastrophe becomes inseparable from a later act of testimony.

1940

Entry into Auschwitz

Pilecki enters the camp under a false identity. The work treats this as a moral threshold, not an adventure motif.

1940-1943

Resistance and reports

Underground organization, intelligence gathering and transmission turn observation into responsibility.

27 Apr 1943

Escape

The escape matters because testimony must leave the place designed to silence it.

1945

Longer report

The later written account deepens the record and becomes part of how the world can still receive the warning.

25 May 1948

Execution

The post-war machinery of accusation and silence becomes another chapter in the pressure placed on truth.

Today

The viewer

The report matters because it asks later generations whether they can still recognize truth under pressure.
