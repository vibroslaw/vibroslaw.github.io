WHAT YOU ARE ABOUT TO WATCH

A guide to music, image and historical memory.

RAP-ORT: Truth of Conscience is an audiovisual work shaped by music, image, testimony and historical memory. It uses artistic form to help the viewer approach a difficult record without pretending to replace historical study.

The guide exists after the screening because emotion alone is not enough. It offers orientation for English-speaking viewers, including the Polish diaspora in Sydney, so the story can be understood beyond Poland as a question about conscience, responsibility and public memory.

01

Memory

The work returns to a record made under extreme pressure and asks how memory is carried across generations, languages and countries.

02

Testimony

In this project, report means testimony, responsibility and moral record. It is not only a document, but a demand placed on the receiver.

03

Reflection

The guide separates historical context from artistic interpretation so the viewer can leave with a clearer question, not a simplified answer.

WHY PILECKI MATTERS

A life at the crossing of courage, testimony and memory.

This section gives only the frame needed for the audiovisual work. Fuller study belongs with the institutions listed below.

I

Courage under occupation

Pilecki's wartime story belongs to a wider history of occupied Poland, clandestine resistance and the attempt to record crimes while they were still unfolding.

II

Testimony under pressure

The reports matter because they show testimony as action. The work asks what it means to speak, write and carry evidence when doing so may cost a life.

III

Memory after repression

After the war, his memory was suppressed under the communist system in Poland. After 1989, public memory could return more openly through research, education and commemoration.

TIMELINE: ORIENTATION ARTIFACT

A map of decisions, testimony and memory.

This is not a full biography. It is a museum-style orientation line: dates, points, places and short notes that help the viewer read the work after the projection.

  1. Witold Pilecki is born in Olonets, then within the Russian Empire.

  2. Józef Cyrankiewicz is born in Tarnów. In the project, his later path becomes a careful, historically sensitive counterpoint.

  3. Poland regains independence after 123 years of partitions.

  4. Germany and the Soviet Union invade Poland. Occupation and clandestine resistance begin.

  5. Pilecki, under the false identity Tomasz Serafiński, deliberately enters a German roundup.

  6. Pilecki is imprisoned in KL Auschwitz and registered as prisoner number 4859.

  7. Inside KL Auschwitz, Pilecki organizes OW — Military Organization, supporting prisoners and transmitting information outside the camp.

  8. Józef Cyrankiewicz arrives at Auschwitz and receives prisoner number 62933.

  9. Pilecki escapes Auschwitz with Jan Redzej and Edward Ciesielski.

  10. Pilecki takes part in the Warsaw Uprising and is later held as a German POW.

  11. After the war, Poland is shaped by Soviet domination; Pilecki returns and conducts information work.

  12. Pilecki is executed in Mokotów Prison in Warsaw.

  13. After the fall of communism, Pilecki’s memory can return more fully to public space.

  14. The question of the report passes to the viewer: what will you do with truth once you know it?

PEOPLE BEHIND THE STORY

Biographies, witnesses and communities of memory.

This section does not turn people into slogans. It separates historical persons, collectives, sources and symbolic figures used in the work.

WP

Witold Pilecki

Officer, prisoner no. 4859, OW organizer and author of testimony

In the project he is the centre of responsibility: the man before the camp, prisoner, organizer of help, author of the report and victim of postwar communist violence.

MS

Maria, Zofia and Andrzej

Family as a dimension of biography

The family reminds viewers that Pilecki’s story does not begin with the camp and does not end with the trial. Behind the report stands a concrete private life.

OW

OW / Military Organization

A network of help, information and resistance inside KL Auschwitz

The guide uses OW according to the source terminology. Some studies use variant names and abbreviations, including ZOW.

JC

Józef Cyrankiewicz

Prisoner no. 62933 and later political figure in communist Poland

This section requires restrained language: confirmed facts are separated from artistic contrast around survival, memory and power.

SK

Stanisław Kłodziński and camp notes

Evidence of clandestine communication and memory

Clandestine notes show that the camp was also a space of hidden information, language, risk and attempts to preserve a human voice.

JR

Jan Redzej and Edward Ciesielski

Pilecki’s escape companions

The escape of 26/27 April 1943 was not a solitary gesture. The guide should underline cooperation and shared risk.

AR

Archivists, researchers, families

Those who restore documents to memory

Without archives, museums, families, historians and editors of sources, the report would be far less accessible to future generations.

V

Viewer

The final link of memory

The viewer does not replace the witness. But the viewer can decide whether known truth becomes responsibility or only a passing emotion.

GLOSSARY

Terms that protect the precision of the story.

No name catalogue. Only the essential terms a viewer needs in order not to lose the historical and linguistic context.

AK / Home Army

The main armed resistance force of the Polish Underground State during the Second World War.

Auschwitz I / Birkenau

Pilecki entered KL Auschwitz / Auschwitz I in 1940. Birkenau and the mass killing function require separate chronological explanation.

Clandestine note / gryps

A short secret prison or underground message, carried at great risk.

KL / Konzentrationslager

German for concentration camp. In the guide it remains part of the language of the perpetrators and the Nazi system.

Łapanka / roundup

A street roundup carried out by German occupation forces.

MBP / UB

Communist security apparatus in postwar Poland, connected with political repression.

NKWD / NKVD

Soviet security police involved in repression across Soviet-controlled territories.

Prisoner number

Part of the camp system, replacing a name with a number and intensifying dehumanisation.

OW / Military Organization

The project’s chosen term for Pilecki’s clandestine organization inside KL Auschwitz. Secondary literature may use variants, including ZOW.

PPS

Polish Socialist Party; an important biographical context for Józef Cyrankiewicz.

PRL / Polish People’s Republic

The communist state system in Poland after the Second World War until 1989.

Report W / Witold’s Report

Pilecki’s source testimonies concerning KL Auschwitz. In the project, “report” also carries a separate moral metaphor.

Rotmistrz

A Polish cavalry rank often rendered in English as cavalry captain.

SS

A Nazi organization central to terror, camp administration and mass murder.

Polish Underground State

Clandestine civil, military and political structures preserving state continuity under occupation.

ZWZ

Union of Armed Struggle, an earlier Polish underground military organization preceding the Home Army.
