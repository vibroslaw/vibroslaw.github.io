REPORT JOURNEY MAP

A restrained interpretive map of how the work moves from testimony toward present-day reflection.

This page does not claim to reconstruct every historical step. It offers an institutional guide to the moral and narrative journey behind Rap-Ort: Prawda Sumienia: witness, report, silence, memory, screening and responsibility.

Why this map exists

For audiences who need orientation before or after a screening.

The audiovisual work is not built as a lecture. It moves through music, image, written fragments and silence. This map helps institutions, educators and international audiences understand the path without reducing the work to a simple timeline.

It is especially useful before a screening, after a discussion, or when explaining the project to people who may not yet know the historical and moral frame of Pilecki’s testimony.

The journey

Seven interpretive stations, each requiring restraint.

1. Witness

The work begins from the idea that truth requires someone willing to see, remember and carry testimony under pressure.

2. Entry

The Auschwitz mission is treated as a moral decision and historical anchor, not as spectacle or adventure.

3. Report

The report becomes a symbol of testimony moving outward from a place designed to isolate, silence and destroy.

4. Transmission

The work asks what happens when truth depends on fragile channels, human courage and the willingness of others to listen.

5. Silence

Silence appears as pressure, political reality, personal fear and the long shadow of memory that was not always allowed to speak.

6. Return of memory

The journey moves toward later remembrance, education and the responsibility to approach the past without distortion.

7. Viewer

The final station is not applause. It is the viewer’s own question: what does responsibility for truth mean now?

After the screening

The map can support a short discussion, a written reflection, a classroom prompt or an institutional programme note.

How institutions can use it

A bridge between the artwork and careful discussion.

Before screening

Use the map as a calm orientation tool. It can prepare viewers without explaining away the experience.

After screening

Use the stations as prompts for discussion about testimony, silence, responsibility and memory.

For mixed audiences

Use the English version as a bridge for viewers who need context before entering a Polish-language work.

Discussion prompts

Questions that keep the conversation serious and open.

Witness
What does it mean to become responsible for something one has seen?
Report
Why does testimony need both courage and a listener?
Silence
When is silence forced, when is it chosen, and when does it become dangerous?
Memory
How can artistic work support remembrance without replacing historical scholarship?
Today
What does the viewer carry after the screening ends?

Presentation guardrails

The map is interpretive, not a substitute for historical study.

Use with care

Do not present this map as a full historical chronology. It is an interpretive route through the work’s moral structure.

Avoid overclaiming

Do not use the map to imply official endorsement, complete source coverage or simplified conclusions about complex history.

Next step
