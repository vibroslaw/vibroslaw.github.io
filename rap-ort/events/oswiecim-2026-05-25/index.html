FACILITATOR MODE

The rhythm after the screening

This page does not replace the facilitator. It provides a structure for conversation and space for student judgement. The most important elements are pauses, questions and their own words.

Workshop principle

We are not reviewing a film. We are not taking a knowledge test. We are working with the question: what remains in a person after encountering testimony?

POST-SCREENING WORKSHOP

Five fields of conversation

Each module contains an opening thought, a central question, discussion prompts and space for an individual response.

Module 01

Contemporary patriotism

Not every form of patriotism begins with grand words. Sometimes it begins with responsibility for truth, memory and language.

Is patriotism today more a declaration, or a responsibility?

  1. Can memory itself be a form of action?
  2. How can we distinguish mature patriotism from performative patriotism?
  3. Does love of community require critical thinking?
Module 02

Moral choices

History becomes real when we see a person in the moment of decision: with fear, risk and no guarantee of a good ending.

When does silence also become a choice?

  1. What separates courage from lack of awareness of risk?
  2. Does a moral choice always have to be visible to others?
  3. How do we recognise the cost of truth before it is too late?
Module 03

Historical memory

Memory is not decoration for the past. It is responsibility for precision, sources, tone and the limits of narration.

Can memory without knowledge be honest?

  1. Why does form influence how history is understood?
  2. How can difficult history be told without simplification?
  3. What does it mean to remember without appropriating suffering?
Module 04

Individual responsibility

Great history often passes through one human being: a word, a signature, a decision, a refusal or silence.

Can one person carry a responsibility greater than their own life?

  1. Where does the viewer's responsibility toward testimony begin?
  2. Does inaction also leave a trace?
  3. What does it mean to carry the question forward?
Module 05

New narrative tools and AI

New tools can help tell history, but they do not remove the author's responsibility for meaning, sources and restraint.

When does technology serve memory, and when does it flatten it?

  1. Can AI support historical education without simplifying tragedy?
  2. Who is responsible for the image of history created with digital tools?
  3. What boundaries should an image have when it touches memory?
Final

Witness Report

This is not a survey. This is not a test. It is a trace of what remains after the screening.

What one sentence do you carry with you?

  1. One sentence that remains.
  2. One question that cannot be postponed.
  3. One responsibility after encountering testimony.
